Authorization — you just saw this.

Your card gets approved in ~1.4 seconds. HDFC puts a hold on ₹4,200 of your credit limit. No money has actually moved. Swiggy hasn't received anything yet — they just got a "yes, good for it."

Think of it like a hotel blocking your card for the room rate at check-in. The charge isn't final — it's a reservation of funds.
📦

Clearing — end of business day

At the end of each day, Swiggy's system batches up every transaction from the day and submits them to Razorpay (their gateway). Razorpay packages these into a clearing file and sends it through Visa to each issuing bank.

This is when HDFC gets the actual transaction details — exactly what was bought, when, and for how much. They match it against the authorization hold from earlier.

This is also why your statement sometimes shows a transaction one day but it clears your available balance a day earlier — the hold and the clear are two different events.
🏦

Settlement — 1 to 3 days later

Now the money actually moves. HDFC transfers funds to Visa's settlement bank. Visa nets out all the transactions between banks and moves the right amounts to each acquirer. Razorpay deposits the net amount into Swiggy's account.

But here's the part that changes the number: the Merchant Discount Rate. Swiggy doesn't get ₹4,200. They get slightly less.

The MDR · Merchant Discount Rate

Every transaction is a four-way split.

When you pay ₹4,200, Swiggy doesn't receive ₹4,200. The MDR — roughly 1.5–2% — is taken out before the merchant sees a rupee. Here's who gets what.

The Real World · What this means for you

The "3% credit card charge" explained.

You've seen how MDR works. Now here's where it shows up in your life — and what you can do about it.

The scenario

You're at an electronics showroom. ₹80,000 TV. You pull out your credit card. The salesperson says:

"Sir, credit card pe 3% extra lagega."
TV price ₹80,000
Their actual MDR (cost to merchant) ~₹1,200–1,600
What they're charging you (3%) ₹2,400
The gap (their markup on the charge) ₹800–1,200
What's actually happening

The MDR is a contract between the merchant and their bank — you are not party to it. The merchant chose to accept cards. The 3% surcharge isn't a fee you legally owe; it's the merchant passing their cost of acceptance onto you — and often marking it up.

This is a grey zone. RBI guidelines discourage surcharging, but enforcement is weak. It happens constantly on big-ticket purchases — jewellery, electronics, car accessories.

Your three moves
💳
Pay UPI
0% MDR. Zero surcharge. Instant. The merchant actually prefers this — no fee, no 3-day wait for settlement.
🤝
Negotiate
MDR is their cost, not yours. Ask them to split the 3% — or waive it. On ₹80,000, they're motivated. "Aap 1.5% lo, main 1.5% deta hoon" is a reasonable ask.
📊
Do the math first
If your card gives 5% cashback on electronics, paying the 3% surcharge still nets you 2% back. Run the numbers before deciding. Sometimes the surcharge is worth it.
